The model is driven by five CICFlowMeter statistics: inter-arrival timing (`Flow IAT Min`=11, SHAP +0.097; `Bwd IAT Min`=506, +0.081) and packet-length extremes (`Packet Length Min`=0, +0.069; `Packet Length Max`=24, +0.069; `Bwd Packet Length Min`=0, +0.066). The fingerprint is a small-packet/header-dominated flow with very short minimum inter-arrival times and zero-length backward packets—consistent with a flooding/scanning-family behavior, not a single named attack class. The detector is binary with confidence 1.00, so it does not identify the class; the SHAP contributions are small individually but aggregate to the flag.

These are statistical flow signatures only. They do not prove IEC-104 protocol semantics, specific commands, or a specific attack action. The model cannot distinguish a deliberate flood from other small-packet traffic; the destination is the RTU-1 IEC-104 endpoint, but port 2404 is not among the top features. The evidence supports “anomalous small-packet timing flow,” not “confirmed protocol-level attack.”
