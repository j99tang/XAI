The intrusion alert targets RTU-1 (IP 10.0.0.1), a BES Cyber Asset under NERC CIP, used for IEC-104 control of critical breakers at Bus 2. The destination port 2404 confirms an IEC-104 endpoint, consistent with a BES Cyber System. The IDS detection of a sustained attack flow, characterized by anomalous packet timing and lengths, constitutes an *attempted* compromise of a BES Cyber Asset. Per NERC CIP-008-6, an attempted intrusion into a BES Cyber System is a Reportable Cyber Security Incident, regardless of success.

Notification is required to the E-ISAC and, under US jurisdiction, NCCIC/CISA. Since this is an *attempted* compromise, the reporting timeline allows notification by the end of the next calendar day. Required attributes—including functional impact, attack vector, and level of intrusion—must be provided, even if partially unknown at initial reporting.

This response falls under the **Detect** function of the NIST CSF 2.0 framework, as it involves identification of a potential incident via network monitoring. The assessment is illustrative; the synthetic grid and European testbed origin mean NERC CIP obligations are applied for demonstration, not as a jurisdictional determination.
